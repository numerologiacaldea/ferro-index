In short

The Ferro Index measures whether a hotel is truly luxury without using reviews: 23 questions about facts you can observe during a stay, weighted the way professional inspectors weight them, a 0-100 score in four bands.

Attention to guests is worth 40 percent on its own. That is not a preference: Forbes Travel Guide states that service carries 70 points out of 100 of the rating.

It was built by Mattia Ferro, founder of Luxury Camp at Union Lido, called an innovator of Italian high-end tourism by Forbes Life.

The method

How true luxury is measured

Reviews measure everyone's satisfaction. The Ferro Index measures something else: whether what you paid for was luxury, or a stage set.

The difference is in what you look at. Not opinions: facts. They called you by name, unprompted. Something arrived before you asked. Your room number written down, never said out loud. Things you watched happen. Or not happen.

Each fact belongs to one of seven pillars. The weights aren't taste: they follow what the major inspection systems have already proven in the field.

Three rules keep the score honest

Flaws weigh more than extras: every false note you mark takes four points off, because one flat no cancels ten courtesies.

Thresholds can't be bought: if attention or cleanliness falls under half, the score stays out of the top bands. Whatever the marble.

Emotion doesn't score: the last question is a cross-check. Facts make the number, your gut confirms it.

The proof

Two rooms at the same price, nineteen points apart

An example built to show the arithmetic. Not two real hotels.

The first has everything that photographs well. Comfort 12 out of 12, design 8 out of 8, art 5 out of 5. But nobody called you by name away from the desk, the preference you mentioned at dinner died there, the problem you reported got passed to a colleague. Attention 14 out of 40. The sum would be 66, but attention is under half and the threshold caps it at 64. Expensive.

The second has no spa and little on the walls. Design 3 out of 8, art 1 out of 5. But your coffee arrives the way you take it from day two, your room number was written down and never spoken, and the lukewarm shower was fixed by the same person you told. Attention 38 out of 40. That makes 83. An excellent hotel, two points off Sanctuary.

Same rate, nineteen points apart. Neither of those things would have shown up in the reviews.

The premise

Why no reviews

Reviews average very different expectations. People who rarely travel reward surprise. People who travel constantly spot fake luxury in twenty minutes: they don't complain, they don't write, they simply never come back. The score you read online mixes the two.

The Ferro Index asks one thing only: what happened to you. If the facts are there, the luxury is there. If they're not, no average of strangers can put them there.

The ratings

Stars, Diamonds, Keys: what they actually certify

A star is not a verdict on your stay. It is an inventory of what a hotel owns.

Italian law uses that exact word. The decree of 21 October 2008 sets national minimum standards for «the services and the fittings» behind the star rating. Square metres, opening hours, languages, lifts: things you count once, and they stay counted.

Below, the systems are lined up along one axis only: how much they look at the inventory, and how much they look at the stay. Move through the list and the judgement stops counting what is there and starts watching what happens.

Who awards hotel stars, and who checks them

In Italy, the regions do. Not the state. The decree issued by the Presidency of the Council of Ministers on 21 October 2008, published in the Gazzetta Ufficiale on 11 February 2009, only sets national minimum standards. Article 2 lets regions and autonomous provinces raise them. Article 5 hands those same regions the rulebooks, the checks, the certificate and the ongoing supervision.

There is a second clause almost nobody reads. Article 3 ties those standards to «the opening of new hotels or the renovation of existing ones», and for hotels already trading it adds that «any obligation to bring structural requirements up to standard is excluded». A historic hotel's star can rest on a building permit from thirty years ago.

What is the difference between a four-star and a five-star hotel

In the Italian national schedule, less than you would guess. The minimum goes from fifteen to sixteen square metres in a double room, from four to five in the bathroom, from two to three foreign languages, from sixteen to twenty-four hours of reception. Add parking for eighty per cent of the rooms instead of fifty, and a service lift.

These are floors, and they apply to new builds and renovations. Regions can ask for more. Between one star and five, the double room grows by two square metres, from fourteen to sixteen.

Do hotel stars mean the same thing in every country

They do not, and this is the part that surprises people outside Europe. Over twenty European countries belong to the Hotelstars Union and, in the Union's own words, «classify their hotels on the basis of the same criteria», with procedures aligned to HOTREC principles to guarantee «transparency and comparability of the stars awarded».

Outside that agreement, everyone sets their own. It is the Hotelstars Union itself that explains France and Portugal run national systems of their own, that Spain has seventeen on a regional basis, and that Italy reaches twenty-one, one per region or autonomous province, each carrying the force of law. Italy and France appear on the Union's list as observers. So a four-star in Trentino and a four-star in Sicily answer to two different rulebooks, and neither answers to the one in Vienna.

What are the Michelin Keys

The MICHELIN Guide's distinction for hotels, announced on 5 October 2023 and awarded for the first time in France in April 2024. Five stated criteria: architecture and interior design, quality and consistency of service, personality and character, coherence between the experience and the price, and what the hotel adds to its destination.

On 8 October 2025, in Paris, the first worldwide selection arrived: 2,457 hotels, of which 1,742 with one Key, 572 with two and 143 with three. Two of those five criteria watch consistency of service and character rather than inventory. That is why, among public systems, the Keys come closest to describing a stay.

Is a five-star hotel automatically a luxury hotel

No, and the word «luxury» never appears in the 2008 decree. Five stars certify fittings, checked when you apply and supervised afterwards. What happens between you and the people working there sits outside that list.

Whoever drafted that decree had seen it too. Article 6 set up «a rating system, linkable to the stars, allowing the measurement and evaluation of the quality of service provided to customers», voluntary for hotels. Article 8 left its parameters to a later implementing act, which does not appear to have been adopted.

What I do, and what I do not

The Ferro Index does not inspect anonymously. I have no inspectors, I buy no stays, I do not walk into other people's hotels with a notebook. I ask the person who was already there about twenty-three things they saw with their own eyes.

That is a limit, and I will name it before you do: a guest sees one stay, an inspector sees a hundred. In exchange, the guest was standing there at two in the morning, when the reception has twenty-four hours of cover on paper and one person awake.

The foundations

What the method stands on

  • Forbes Travel Guide: up to 900 standards verified incognito, one by one, yes or no. Forbes states that service carries 70 points out of 100 of the rating, the property 30.
  • LQA, Leading Quality Assurance: over 1,000 standards per stay, alongside a reading of how the service felt. The report goes to the hotel, not the guest.
  • Michelin Keys: five stated criteria, two of which watch consistency of service and character rather than fittings.
  • AAA Five Diamond: requirements that rule you out before scoring even starts, checked by unannounced annual inspections.
  • Academic research: from SERVQUAL onwards, the studies keep repeating the same thing. How people behave predicts perceived quality better than any material.

The Ferro Index translates those systems into questions a guest can answer alone, and filters them through one precise idea: luxury is made of time, exclusivity and love. Everything else is price.

Questions

What everyone asks

How can I tell if a hotel is truly luxury?

Look at facts, not declared stars: they call you by name unprompted, something arrives before you ask, your room number is written down and never said out loud, the juice is squeezed to order. The Ferro Index asks 23 questions about these facts and turns them into a score.

Why aren't reviews enough?

Because they average people who are amazed by everything with people who never complain. True luxury isn't judged by other people's average: it's verified with your own facts.

What matters most?

Attention: 40 percent of the score. That's the proportion used by professional systems, not a personal taste. Materials can be bought in an afternoon; a service that anticipates takes years to build.

What is it, in one sentence?

The luxury inspectors' method, in guest form: three minutes, twenty-three facts, one number that doesn't fall for the show.

Can I share the result?

Yes. The score travels inside the link: whoever receives it sees the verdict, the radar and the detail without retaking the test. Nothing gets stored anywhere: the answers live only in the address you decide to send.
